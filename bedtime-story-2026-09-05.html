小星星暖暖的陪伴之夜（胎教期）

夜空中，暖黄的小星星暖暖眨眨眼睛，忽然看见窗台上有一株小苗，正随着晚风轻轻摇。小苗的叶子小小的，沾着一点露珠，在月光下亮晶晶的。「它是不是有点冷呀？」暖暖想，就飘了下来，蹲在小苗旁边，轻轻说：「我陪着你呀。」

小苗抬起头，看见暖暖圆滚滚、亮闪闪的样子，好奇地问：「你是月亮掉下来的一小块吗？」暖暖笑了：「我是星星呀，专门来陪你说话的。」小苗高兴地摇了摇叶子：「太好了，我还以为这个晚上只有我和风呢。」

晚风哗啦地吹过，小苗又抖了抖。暖暖想了想，把自己身上的光调得再亮一点、再暖一点，像一个圆圆的小灯笼，把小苗整个罩进暖光里。「现在不冷了吧？」暖暖问。小苗舒服地舒展叶子：「不冷了，你的光像小被子一样。」

蛐蛐蛐蛐，草丛里传来细细的叫声。小苗好奇地问：「那是什么呀？」「是蟋蟀在唱歌，」暖暖说，「它们和星星是好朋友，晚上总会为彼此唱一首歌。」说着，暖暖也轻轻哼起来，滴滴答答，像小小的风铃在夜里摇。小苗听着听着，不抖了。

半夜里，小苗打了个小小的哈欠。「困了吗？」暖暖问。「有一点，」小苗说，「可是我怕我一睡着，你就不见了。」暖暖把光收成一圈，像星星们手拉手围成一个小小的摇篮，轻轻摇着小苗：「我一直在呢。你睡吧，我数着露珠陪你。」

小苗慢慢睡着了，叶子轻轻起伏，像在做甜甜的梦。暖暖守在小苗身边，一夜都没有眨走它的光。天快亮的时候，暖暖轻轻说：「我要回去啦，不过明天晚上，我还来陪你。」小苗在梦里听见了，叶子微微弯了弯，像在说：明天见。

小宝宝，你也在妈妈肚子里，被一片温暖的光轻轻包裹着。那就是我们的陪伴，像小星星暖暖一样，一直在，一直暖。等你准备好了，外面的世界也有许多愿意陪着你的人。现在，安心睡吧，我们陪着你呀。

✨ 故事小语
陪伴是最温柔的力量，有人陪着，什么都不怕。

🔬 🔬科学故事（胎教期）：雪花的六瓣小衣裳

冬天到了，天上飘下亮晶晶的小雪花。宝宝如果将来用手心接住一朵，仔仔细细地看，会发现雪花虽然一片一片长得都不一样，却几乎都穿着「六瓣的小衣裳」——有的像六角的小星星，有的像六瓣的小花。这到底是为什么呢？

秘密藏在雪花长出来的地方。雪花是在云朵里的一颗小小冰晶上开始长的。冰晶是由水分子手拉手排成的，而水分子手拉手的时候，天生就爱排成六个角的形状——就像六个小伙伴，手拉手围成一圈，正好是六边形。所以，每一片雪花的小骨架，从一开始就是六角形的。

云朵里的水汽，一片一片围到这颗六角的小骨架上，有的地方长得多一点，有的地方少一点。风一吹，温度一变，它们就长出不同的花纹：有的伸出六根长长的胳膊，像星星；有的在中间开出小小的花心，像花朵。科学家把雪花放在放大镜下拍照，发现世界上几乎没有两片雪花长得一模一样——每一片，都是独一无二的小艺术品。

有趣的是，如果天气暖一点，水汽贴在雪花上，会化掉棱角；如果天气冷一点，水汽又会在角上长出新枝。所以雪花的花纹，其实是云朵里温度和水汽的「日记」，每一片都记着它在云里旅行的故事。

所以呀，宝宝，等将来冬天飘雪的时候，你可以和爸爸妈妈一起，伸出小手套，接住一片雪花，数一数它的小衣裳是不是六瓣的。每一片都不同，每一片都好看——就像你，也是这个世界上独一无二的宝贝，正等着在爱的雪花里，慢慢长大。

✨ 故事小语
雪花几乎都是六角形的，因为冰晶里水分子手拉手的排列方式天然是六边形；水汽围着这颗六角的小骨架慢慢长大，就开出六瓣的花。而每朵雪花的图案，又是独一无二的。

Conch and the Sound of the Sea (Prenatal)

Little Conch lived in the deep-sea seagrass forest, quiet and gentle and full of curiosity. "So that is how it is..." it would murmur, tapping its shell. One evening, as the sea turned silver, Conch wondered: "Where does all the sound in the sea come from?"

So Conch floated out of the seagrass and asked the sea itself. Tinkle went a bubble rising up, past Conch's ear. "That is a hello from the sand," whispered the sea. Softly, softly, another bubble went tinkle, tinkle, and Conch said, "So that is how sand says hello!"

Conch swam to the coral reef, where a little fish was flicking its tail. Boom-boom went the old drum coral, deep and slow. "That is the reef's heartbeat," said the fish. "It has been beating for a very, very long time, so everyone in the sea knows they are home." Conch tapped its shell gently, learning the rhythm.

A whale's song drifted from far away, long and low, like a lullaby that wrapped around everything. "That song travels through the whole sea," said the fish, "all the way to the baby whales. It tells them someone is thinking of them."

Conch floated home, full of sounds. Back in the seagrass forest, it pressed its shell against a warm stone and listened. From inside the shell came the softest echo — whoosh, tinkle, boom — as if the whole sea had come to live inside it. "So that is how it is," Conch smiled. "Every sound is someone saying hello, and saying I am here with you."

That night, a cloud-soft little blanket of moonbeams tucked over the forest, wrapping Conch in a small, warm cocoon. "Little one in Mommy's tummy," Conch whispered, "can you hear the sea singing? It is singing for you too. And so is everyone who loves you."

Little one, when you are ready, the world will sound like the sea: full of hellos and heartbeats and songs that travel far just to reach you. You are wrapped in sound and love right now, growing safe and sound. Listen — someone is thinking of you, always.

✨ 故事小语
Every sound in the world is someone's way of saying hello — and of saying I am here with you.

🔬 🔬 Science Story (Prenatal): Why Snowflakes Have Six Sides

In winter, bright little snowflakes drift down from the sky. If you ever catch one on your mitten and look very closely, you will see that snowflakes come in countless shapes — yet almost every one wears six little sides. Some look like six-pointed stars, some like tiny flowers. Where does the six come from?

The secret begins where a snowflake is born. Every snowflake starts as a tiny speck of ice high inside a cloud. Ice is made of water molecules holding hands in a special pattern, and water molecules love to hold hands in sixes — like six small friends joining hands in a ring, making a hexagon. So from the very first moment, a snowflake's little skeleton is six-sided.

Then water vapor in the cloud gathers around that six-sided skeleton, arm by arm. The wind blows, the temperature changes, and the crystal grows little branches in different places: six long arms reaching out like a star, or a tiny flower opening at the center. Scientists photograph snowflakes under magnifying lenses and find that almost no two snowflakes are exactly alike — each one is a tiny, one-of-a-kind work of art.

Here is another wonder: if the air is a little warmer, the vapor melts the crystal's sharp edges; if it is colder, new branches sprout at the tips. So the shape of a snowflake is like a diary of its journey through the cloud — every branch records the temperature and moisture it met along the way.

So, little one, one day when winter comes, you can hold out your mitten with Mama and Papa and catch a snowflake of your own. Count its six little sides, and know this: each flake is different, and each is beautiful. And so are you — the one and only you, growing safe and warm, waiting to drift gently into this world of ours.

✨ 故事小语
Almost all snowflakes are six-sided because water molecules lock together in six-sided patterns; water vapor builds around that six-pointed skeleton into delicate arms. And yet no two snowflakes are ever exactly alike.
